Local coastal support and air priority — Runtime 1.8.6

A friendly coastal battery may task a searchlight only when the battery's unit
position is at most 2,000 metres from that trailer. This is the battery-to-lamp
support radius; ships still use the lamp's existing beam range and line of sight.
Requests retain estimated target positions, gradual physical traverse, sustained
optical acquisition, smoke occlusion, low-light restrictions and one lamp token
per target. Daylight does not activate searchlights for coastal support.

Aircraft have priority:
- A lamp already assigned to aircraft explicitly declines coastal work.
- An available friendly radar air cue can interrupt an active ship assignment.
- An enemy aircraft encountered within the actual beam can interrupt ship work.
- Idle lamps consider radar and beam aircraft cues before accepting coastal tasks.
- Taking an air assignment immediately releases ship illumination and its token,
  allowing another free nearby lamp to take over. Coastal work can resume after
  the air assignment ends. Existing aircraft claims prevent duplicate assignments.

Ongoing ship assignments also require a valid nearby requesting battery. Moving
or disabling the battery/trailer can revoke the assignment. Multiple batteries
requesting the same ship are filtered individually by distance; a newer distant
request cannot hide an eligible nearby request. An out-of-range lamp does not
hold up that battery's optional area-flare fallback.
