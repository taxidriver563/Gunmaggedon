Problem
The native BlastFrag call with yield 1 searches only a 20m radius. Native
armor further limits useful blast damage, while F88 shots intentionally cover
a much larger aerial volume. Increasing yield alone also increases close-range
blast power, so it does not independently balance a broad fragment envelope.
The one-gun barrage pattern also had a fixed offset toward one side.

Behavior
- Retains the native close blast, then performs one additional instantaneous
  aircraft-fragment pass out to 140m from the actual timed airburst.
- Additional fragment damage falls off as (1 - distance/140)^0.75; zero at or
  beyond the boundary. Distances use collider surfaces, not aircraft centers.
- Maximum additional native part-HP budget per aircraft: 12 times falloff.
  At most three exposed parts, each capped at 4 times its own falloff.
  The existing close blast is separate and is not covered by these outer caps.
- Fragment penetration is capped at native value 140. Strong armor can block
  fragments; native pierce tolerance and armor are used to limit each hit.
  Calls go through IDamageable.TakeDamage, retaining damage attribution,
  native aircraft damage handling and RPCs; no direct HP assignment is used.
- Terrain, hulls and other intervening colliders block fragment paths. Each
  part is considered once despite multiple colliders. Budgets are reserved
  before callbacks, and no aircraft receives more than three part hits/pass.
- This adds no smoke damage or timed damage-over-time component. Repeated
  shell bursts accumulate damage. Fragments are physical hazards to friendly
  aircraft too; they do not use an enemy-only damage filter.
- Damage requires authoritative timed expiry and server/local-simulation
  aircraft. Remote presentation messages remain visual only.
- Ground/ship units retain only the existing native core blast behavior.
- Reusable per-call pooled overlap buffers, part candidates and aircraft
  budgets avoid a per-frame search. Buffers grow on saturation; an allocating
  fallback preserves all targets if the 1024-collider buffer is full. Nested
  damage callbacks receive independent scratch and references clear on return.

Other F88 improvements
- Custom controller yaw: 12 -> 16 degrees/sec; elevation: 10 -> 12 degrees/sec.
  Remote pose interpolation uses the same rates. These runtime overrides do
  not change the prefab's native/manual-control speed fields.
- Plan preparation begins with up to 1.55s of reload remaining (was 1.05s),
  allowing more time for three aim samples before the breech becomes ready.
- A lone gun now centers its random barrage around the committed estimate;
  multiple guns retain distinct overlapping sector offsets.
- Mechanical dispersion 0.65 degrees and fuze error +/-0.18s are unchanged.
  Observation error, radar's knowledge-only advantage, committed-area fire,
  night illumination requirements and full 6.2s mechanical reload remain.

LOD and assets

sounds, particles and loading animation assets remain from pack 0.0.26.
The 800-check LOD integration suite passed with the new runtime.
